Install

macOS

  • Download the signed and notarized DMG from Releases, drag EchoDiar to Applications.
  • Needs macOS 15+ on Apple Silicon. Apple Speech and Apple Foundation Models need macOS 26.
  • EchoDiar lives in the menu bar. Its Dock icon shows while one of its windows is open.
  • Updates itself: menu bar icon > Check for Updates…

Windows

Why the extra step? Installing an app on Windows usually takes one click. EchoDiar is not yet signed by a public certificate authority, so Windows first needs you to trust EchoDiar's own certificate (step 2). You do it once per PC: updates install without it. A signed build is planned, which removes this step.

  1. Download EchoDiar.cer and EchoDiar.appinstaller from the Assets of a release that includes Windows, on the Releases page, or directly: EchoDiar.cer and EchoDiar.appinstaller.
  2. Open EchoDiar.cer > Install Certificate... > Local Machine (not the default Current User) > Place all certificates in the following store > Browse... > Trusted People > OK > Next > Finish. Click Open or Yes when Windows asks.
  3. Open EchoDiar.appinstaller and click Install. EchoDiar downloads and opens.
  • Needs Windows 11 on x64 or Arm64.
  • Updates itself through App Installer: the title bar shows Update when a new version is out, and tray icon > Check for updates… checks now.
  • Why Windows warns, what these steps change, and what to do if App Installer refuses to install: Windows warnings.

Windows warnings

  • Why Windows warns: EchoDiar for Windows is signed with EchoDiar's own certificate, not by a public certificate authority, so Windows does not know the publisher until you trust that certificate. The security prompts in the install steps come from that. EchoDiar is open source, and GitHub Actions builds each release from its source.
  • Prompts you will see: Open File - Security Warning (Unknown Publisher) when the certificate opens, This CA Root certificate is not trusted... in the Certificate window, and a permission prompt for Windows host process (Rundll32), verified publisher Microsoft Windows, when you choose Local Machine. They are expected. Never put the certificate in Trusted Root.
  • Check the download (optional): each release has SHA256SUMS-windows.txt. Before you import EchoDiar.cer, you can compare its SHA-256 hash with the EchoDiar.cer line of that file, and the bundle's too if you download it. In PowerShell, in the folder of the download, run the line below (upper or lower case does not matter). 
    Get-FileHash .\EchoDiar.cer -Algorithm SHA256
  • What the steps change: they add one certificate, EchoDiar's, to Local Machine > Trusted People, so Windows accepts the app's signature. That step is the one that asks for Yes. Then App Installer installs EchoDiar for your account from the feed at ed.dinhanhthi.com, which keeps it updated.

If App Installer says the publisher certificate could not be verified

App Installer shows Publisher: Unknown, Install is greyed out, and it says This app package's publisher certificate could not be verified. ... must be verified (0x800B010A). EchoDiar's certificate is not in Local Machine > Trusted People, for example because it went to Current User or another store. Click Cancel, do step 2 of the install steps again, then open EchoDiar.appinstaller again.

If App Installer says a framework or dependency is missing

A general Windows tip, not tested with EchoDiar: open Microsoft Store > Library > Get updates, then open EchoDiar.appinstaller again.

Smart App Control

Smart App Control may block EchoDiar, because the app is not signed by a public certificate authority yet.

After uninstalling

Uninstall EchoDiar in Settings > Apps > Installed apps. Your recordings in %LOCALAPPDATA%\EchoDiar stay. EchoDiar's certificate stays in Trusted People too. To remove it: Start, search "computer certificates", open Manage computer certificates (click Yes when Windows asks for permission), go to Trusted People > Certificates, right-click the certificate issued to Anh-Thi Dinh, click Delete, then Yes.

A signed build is planned

A build signed by a public certificate authority is planned. Windows will then know the publisher, and the certificate step goes away. Moving to it takes one reinstall; your recordings stay.

Permissions

  • Microphone: records your voice. On Windows, if it is off, turn on EchoDiar in Windows Settings > Privacy & security > Microphone.
  • System Audio Recording (on Mac): records the meeting's audio. The first-run onboarding tests it and opens the right Privacy & Security pane. On Windows, the onboarding's Test System Audio checks that EchoDiar hears it.
  • Accessibility (optional, on Mac): only to keep the floating indicator on the meeting window. Windows needs no permission for that.
  • Calendars (optional): reads your meetings to offer recording when they start and to name the recording, and adds the events you create to an "EchoDiar" calendar. EchoDiar writes to no other calendar. On Mac, it reads macOS Calendar, and the "EchoDiar" calendar is On My Mac when available, otherwise in your default account, e.g. iCloud, which syncs it. On Windows, click Grant Access in Settings > Recording > Calendar; if access is off, turn on EchoDiar in Windows Settings > Privacy & security > Calendar.
  • Tell the people you record. Onboarding and Settings > General have a Copy consent message button.

Recording

  • Record one app's audio or all system audio, plus your mic. Mic and system audio go to separate channels of one file.
  • When an app's audio cannot be isolated, EchoDiar records all system audio and says so.
  • On Mac, start or stop with Cmd+R in the app, or Cmd+Option+R from any app. Pause with Cmd+Shift+P in the app, or Cmd+Option+P from any app.
  • On Windows, start or stop with Ctrl+R in the app, or Ctrl+Alt+R from any app. Pause with Ctrl+Shift+P in the app, or Ctrl+Alt+P from any app.
  • Live level meters while recording, and live captions with Transcribe while recording. The menu bar icon on Mac shows idle, recording, paused and processing. On Windows, the notification area icon shows a red dot while recording and an orange badge while paused; a left click opens EchoDiar.
  • Pick the microphone, and what happens when it disconnects, in Settings > Recording.
  • Each recording is compressed to AAC (64 kbps by default) once it is transcribed. A recording cut short by a crash is recovered and transcribed at next launch.

Meeting detection

  • Offers to record when a meeting app starts using the mic: Zoom, Teams, Slack, Webex, Discord, Skype, FaceTime on Mac, and browsers for web calls such as Meet.
  • Per app: Ask, Always record or Ignore (browsers: Ask or Ignore).
  • A floating indicator shows while recording. Drag it, hide it while EchoDiar is in front, or attach it to the meeting window.
  • Auto-stop: when the meeting app quits, the call goes quiet or the screen locks, the indicator asks before stopping, with a 15 s countdown.

Calendar

  • On Mac, works with any account added to macOS Calendar: iCloud, Google, Outlook/Exchange, CalDAV. On Windows, reads the calendars Windows shares with apps. Set it up in Settings > Recording > Calendar, with the optional Calendars permission.
  • When a meeting starts: Off, Ask (the default: the floating indicator offers Record, Join or Not now), Wait for the call (records the meeting app as soon as the call starts) or Record at start (records all system audio and the mic at the start time).
  • The automatic modes start without asking only for events you organize, accepted or answered Maybe to, and events without invitees in the EchoDiar calendar (or, on Mac, a calendar On My Mac). Other events ask.
  • Choose the calendars to watch, and keep only events with a meeting link (Zoom, Google Meet, Microsoft Teams, Webex).
  • Stop when the event ends, plus a few minutes (10 by default). The indicator asks before stopping, with a 15 s countdown.
  • A recording started from an event is named after it.
  • The Calendar page (toolbar button next to Settings) lists the meetings of the next 7 days (updated automatically) with Join, Record and Skip. The menu bar menu on Mac, and the notification area icon's menu on Windows, list upcoming meetings.
  • New Event… on the Calendar page creates a meeting: title, date, start and end, and an optional meeting link (https). It goes to an "EchoDiar" calendar: on Mac, On My Mac when available, otherwise your default account, e.g. iCloud, which syncs those events like any other; on Windows, EchoDiar's own calendar, which no other app can add events to.
  • Repeat: None, Daily, Weekdays, Weekly or Monthly, with End Repeat never or on a date.
  • Edit… on events in the EchoDiar calendar changes them, and Delete Event… is in the editor. For a repeating event, choose This Event Only or This and Future Events; when Repeat or End Repeat changed, Save offers only This and Future Events.
  • Events in the EchoDiar calendar count even without a meeting link, and follow the same When a meeting starts mode. On Mac, don't share the EchoDiar calendar: anyone who can add events to it could start a recording.
  • Works while EchoDiar runs, so turn on Launch at login in Settings > General.

Transcription

  • Starts on its own: while you record with Transcribe while recording, otherwise after Stop. Turn it off with Transcribe automatically in Settings > Recording.
  • Engines on Mac: WhisperKit, Parakeet, Qwen3-ASR, and Apple Speech on macOS 26.
  • Engines on Windows: Whisper (whisper.cpp: Large v3 Turbo by default, Small, Base, Large v3, and PhoWhisper Medium, Vietnamese only) and Parakeet TDT 0.6B v3.
  • Models are downloaded only when you click Download in Settings > Transcription. On Mac, macOS installs Apple Speech language assets the first time you use a language.
  • Speakers: your mic is Me. Turn on Tell speakers apart in Settings > Recording (or Settings > Transcription > Diarization model) to label each voice in the meeting audio: Speaker 1, Speaker 2… Set the number of other speakers there as a hint. Off (the default), the meeting audio is one speaker, Speaker 1, and cloud transcription is asked for no speaker labels.
  • Transcribe while recording (on by default, Whisper and Parakeet): transcribes during the recording and shows live captions, so the transcript is ready seconds after Stop. Uses more CPU and memory while recording. Turn it off in Settings > Transcription > General. See How it works.
  • Re-transcribe a conversation with another engine or language.
  • On Mac, can wait while the Mac is hot or in Low Power Mode: what is left after Stop is done once the Mac cools down or leaves Low Power Mode.
  • On Windows, can wait while energy saver is on, with Wait while energy saver is on (on by default) in Settings > Recording > After recording: what is left after Stop is done once energy saver is off.

How it works

  • With Transcribe while recording on (the default), the recording is transcribed while it happens. Each channel, your mic and the meeting audio, is cut into chunks at pauses in speech, at most 12 s; with no pause, the cut falls on the quietest moment. Each chunk becomes one line of the live transcript. While a chunk is still open, its text so far shows in grey and is refreshed every few seconds; the chunk's line replaces it. When decoding falls behind, on a slower computer, the status says how many seconds behind it is.
  • Chunks wait in a queue that keeps up to about 10 minutes of audio, and one speech model, loaded once for the recording, transcribes them. Its lines are the live captions, Me and Others, and they become the saved transcript. Whisper and Parakeet only.
  • Nothing is lost: chunks beyond the queue and chunks that fail become gaps, transcribed from the recording after Stop. On Mac, chunks held while the Mac is hot or in Low Power Mode (with Wait when the Mac is hot or in Low Power Mode on) stay in the queue and are transcribed once the Mac recovers; chunks still held at Stop become gaps too.

On, the speaker model runs after Stop and labels Speaker 1, Speaker 2…: about 10 s for the same meeting.
  • The transcript is made from the uncompressed audio, which is slightly more accurate: 0.0 % word error rate on the same meeting, against about 1 % from the compressed file.

Cloud transcription

  • Optional: OpenAI, Groq, Mistral, Gemini, ElevenLabs, Deepgram, AssemblyAI or a custom OpenAI-compatible endpoint. Set up ElevenLabs, Deepgram and AssemblyAI in Settings > Transcription > Transcription model, the others in Settings > AI.
  • The first run for each provider and address asks before any audio leaves your computer. A changed URL asks again.
  • The mic and system channels are uploaded, and billed, separately.
  • API keys are stored in the Keychain on Mac, in Credential Manager on Windows.

Transcript & playback

  • Click a line to hear it. Space plays or pauses, Esc goes back.
  • Edit the title, rename speakers, copy the transcript or reveal the folder in Finder on Mac, File Explorer on Windows.

AI summaries & chat

  • Open the AI panel with Cmd+I on Mac, Ctrl+I on Windows. Generate a summary or ask questions about a conversation.
  • Answers cite [mm:ss] timestamps. Click one to jump to that moment in the transcript and recording.
  • Response language, for summaries and answers: automatic (the transcript's language for summaries, the question's for answers), English, French or Vietnamese.
  • On-device: on Mac, MLX models, and Apple Foundation Models on macOS 26. On Windows, GGUF models (Qwen3.5 2B, 4B or 9B, Gemma 4 E2B or E4B), and Phi Silica on Copilot+ PCs.
  • Local servers: LM Studio, Ollama, Jan, llama.cpp, vLLM, plus Foundry Local on Windows. Scan finds the ones running on this computer.
  • Cloud APIs: OpenAI, Anthropic, xAI, Gemini, Mistral, Azure OpenAI, OpenRouter, Groq, DeepSeek or a custom endpoint. Keys go in the Keychain on Mac, in Credential Manager on Windows.
  • Subscription CLIs: Claude Code, Codex, OpenCode or Gemini CLI, with your own login.
  • Copy as prompt copies the transcript and your question, ready to paste into any chat app. On Windows, Ctrl+Shift+C does it from the Summary or Chat tab.

Library & search

  • Conversations grouped by day, with their size on disk.
  • Rename, reveal in Finder (Explorer on Windows) or move to the Trash (the Recycle Bin on Windows).
  • Search titles and transcripts with Cmd+F on Mac, Ctrl+F on Windows.

Storage

  • Data lives in ~/Library/Application Support/EchoDiar on Mac, %LOCALAPPDATA%\EchoDiar on Windows, with the same layout. Settings > Storage shows the space used.
  • Auto-delete: once a day, moves transcribed conversations older than an age you set to the Trash (the Recycle Bin on Windows), whole or audio only.
  • The uncompressed audio (audio.caf) is kept until the transcript is written, then compressed to audio.m4a and deleted. If transcription can't start right away, the audio is compressed first.
  • Optionally delete the audio once a transcription succeeds: with Delete audio after transcription, a new recording's uncompressed audio is moved to the Trash (the Recycle Bin on Windows) instead of being compressed. The transcript, summary and chat stay.
  • Choose the compression bitrate, or keep the uncompressed audio too.

Settings & shortcuts

  • Open with Cmd+, on Mac, Ctrl+, on Windows. Sections: General, Recording, Transcription, AI, RAG, Storage, Shortcuts, About, Acknowledgements.
  • Change the global record and pause shortcuts, and view the app's shortcuts, in Settings > Shortcuts.
  • Launch at login and the floating indicator are in Settings > General.
  • Default language, Remember last record language (off by default: keeps the language picked in the Record button's menu for the next recordings), Transcribe while recording and Free unused models after are in Settings > Transcription > General. Tell speakers apart is in both Settings > Recording and Settings > Transcription > Diarization model.
